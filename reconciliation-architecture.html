Matching engine — decision sequence
STEP 1
Join on reference
Unique ref present on both sides? If only one side → missing-record break.
STEP 2
Duplicate check
Reference appears more than once on a side → duplicate break.
STEP 3
Currency
Must be identical — otherwise FX/treasury break (can't net).
STEP 4
Amount ± tol
Within amount tolerance → ok; beyond → amount-mismatch break.
STEP 5
Status & date
Status must agree; value date within window, else timing break.
STEP 6
Clear or route
All pass → auto-cleared. Any fail → typed exception to worklist.

Break taxonomy

Matched— all fields agree (auto-cleared)
Within tolerance— tiny FX/rounding diff, auto-cleared & logged
Amount mismatch— difference beyond tolerance
Currency mismatch— cannot net; FX/treasury review
Status mismatch— settlement-state conflict
Date-window break— settles outside value-date window
Missing at processor— in ledger, not settled
Missing in ledger— at processor, unrecorded (fraud/mis-book)
Duplicate— double-posted reference

Design principles

  • Reference is the contract. A unique, processor-accepted reference on every txn is the single requirement that makes automated matching possible.
  • Feed a shared layer, don't duplicate it. Normalized output and the ledger of record are consumed by treasury, finance & other product lines.
  • Exceptions are the product. The value isn't the match — it's shrinking and ageing the break queue over time.
  • Auditable & replayable. Raw feeds retained; every clear/break is explainable and re-runnable.
Requirements on each processor: daily report via SFTP or transactions API · a unique, caller-supplied transaction reference accepted & echoed on every record · consistent status vocabulary · fees itemised separately.
