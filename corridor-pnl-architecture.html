Corridor P&L — System Architecture

From per-processor configuration to a corridor-by-corridor view of whether the company is making or losing money.

Configuration
Processor setup
Add a processor; declare supported transaction types (deposit / payout), payment methods (mobile money, virtual account, bank) and MNOs (MTN, Airtel, M-Pesa, Vodacom, Orange…).
Corridor & fee config
Per corridor (country/currency pair): fixed fee, % fee, optional FX fee, and FX markup — captured for both the processor (cost) and the customer (revenue).
Rate & fee engine
Cost model
What the processor charges the company to fulfil: fixed + % + FX markup/fee, per corridor, type and channel.
Revenue model
What the company charges its customer: fixed + % + FX markup, shaped by the fee-treatment choice (absorb / pass-through / pass-through + margin).
Margin calculation
Per-transaction margin
Revenue − cost on a representative transaction, combining the fixed, percentage and FX components.
Scaled by volume
Monthly volume ÷ average size → transaction count; applied to fixed and percentage components to get monthly P&L per corridor.
Dashboard
Corridor P&L
Profit / loss and margin % per corridor, with a clear profitable vs. loss-making flag.
Roll-ups & KPIs
Net margin, blended %, profitable vs. loss count; margin by transaction type and payment method.
The margin equation — per corridor

Cost to company (processor)

  • Fixed fee × transaction count
  • % fee × volume
  • FX markup % × volume (rate processor fulfils at)
  • Optional explicit FX fee % × volume

Revenue to company (customer)

  • Customer fixed fee × transaction count
  • Customer % fee × volume
  • FX markup % × volume (rate offered to customer)
  • Shaped by fee treatment: absorb / pass-through / + margin
Corridor margin = Customer revenue − Processor cost  ·  FX margin = (customer markup % − processor markup %) × volume

Design principles

  • Cost and price are separate inputs. Modelling the processor side and the customer side independently is what makes the margin visible.
  • FX is usually the real margin. The spread between the rate the processor fulfils at and the rate offered to the customer often dwarfs the fee line.
  • Absorbing fees is a decision, not a default. The dashboard makes the cost of "free" corridors explicit.
  • Decide per corridor. Profitability is corridor-by-corridor; a blended average hides the losers.
Primary objective: a visual, corridor-by-corridor answer to one question — is the company profitable on this corridor, given the processor's cost and the price offered to customers?
