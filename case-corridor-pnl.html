The problem

A cross-border business moves money through several processors, each with its own fees, FX rates, payment methods and corridors — and sets its own customer-facing prices on top. Whether any given corridor makes money depends on the gap between what the processor charges the company and what the company charges its customer, across fixed fees, percentage fees and the FX spread.

That gap usually lives in scattered rate cards and people's heads. Customer pricing is set corridor by corridor with no single view of the resulting margin, and FX — normally the biggest lever — is rarely modelled against the processor's fulfilment rate. So the business can't confidently answer a basic question, and loss-making corridors (often where fees are absorbed to drive adoption) hide inside a healthy-looking blended average.

Which corridors are profitable, which are losing money, and by how much — given what the processor costs us and what we charge our customers?

My role

I defined the config data model, the fee and FX margin logic, and the dashboard that turns it into a decision. The artifacts here are the PRD, a working interactive console with a live P&L dashboard, and the system architecture.

Approach

  1. Modelled cost and price as two separate inputsFor every corridor, the console captures the processor's terms (the cost to the company) and the company's customer-facing terms (the revenue) independently. That separation is what makes margin visible at all.
  2. Captured the full fee shapeFixed amount, percentage, or both — plus an optional explicit FX fee — on each side, per corridor, transaction type and payment channel (including MNO for mobile money).
  3. Made FX a first-class leverFX is expressed as a markup over the mid-market rate on both sides, so the rate the processor fulfils at and the rate offered to the customer are directly comparable. The spread is often the real margin.
  4. Made fee treatment explicitAbsorb, pass-through, or pass-through-with-margin is a deliberate choice per corridor — and the dashboard shows exactly what "free" costs.
  5. Scaled per-transaction economics to monthly P&LMonthly volume and average transaction size convert unit economics into real money, so fixed fees bite on low-value/high-count corridors and FX dominates high-value ones.

The margin equation

Corridor margin = customer revenue − processor cost, where each side is fixed fee × transaction count + % fee × volume + FX markup × volume (plus an optional FX fee on the cost side).

FX margin = (customer FX markup % − processor FX markup %) × volume. Expressing both as a markup over the same mid-market rate makes "the rate the processor fulfils at" and "the rate we offer the customer" directly comparable.

The sharp consequence: absorbing fees isn't free, and a positive blended average can hide corridors that lose money on every transaction. The dashboard is built to surface exactly those.
