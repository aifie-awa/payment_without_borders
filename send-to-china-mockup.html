What is a CNAPS code, and why did the first attempt struggle?

CNAPS (China National Advanced Payment System) codes are 12-digit identifiers that route a domestic RMB payment to the exact bank branch. For a cross-border transfer settling in local currency (CNY), the receiving bank needs the correct CNAPS code to credit the right branch.

When Afriex first exposed the CNAPS field, it asked senders to supply that code themselves. But senders were used to sending to China without ever touching a CNAPS code — competitors quietly handle the mapping in the background. Surfacing the raw requirement felt like a step backwards, and adoption was lukewarm.

The fix isn't to remove the requirement — the receiving rail still needs it — but to do the work for the sender. Afriex maps every supported bank, branch and location to its CNAPS and postal code, so the sender just selects from dropdowns. The complexity moves from the customer to the product.
