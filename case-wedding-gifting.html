Case study · Collect in the wild · Group gifting

One wedding, guests on four continents, one payment link

A real-world scenario that stress-tests Collect: many contributors, in many currencies, gifting one couple. The interesting product question wasn't "can we build it?" — it was "is this a new product, or a variation?"

The scenario

A friend was born and raised in Nigeria, where she finished her first degree, then moved to Mauritius for graduate studies. There she met coursemates from all over Africa. When the program ended everyone scattered — some home to Nigeria, Ghana, South Africa; others to jobs in new countries entirely.

She now works in Kenya, and that's where she's getting married. She sends out invites — but many of those Mauritius friends, now spread across the continent and beyond, can't travel to Nairobi. So alongside the invitation she shares a payment link where they can send a wedding gift: a few preconfigured amounts, or a custom amount if they prefer.

Every guest wants to give in the money they actually hold. The couple in Nairobi just wants the gifts to arrive, cleanly, as one predictable pot.

The product question

Before building, the call worth making: is cross-border group gifting a separate product, or a variation of Collect? My test — a scenario earns standalone-product status only if it needs a distinct core flow, data model, or monetization. Against that test:

DimensionWedding gifting
Core flow (link → payer picks currency & channel → pay → settle)Identical to Collect. No change.
Rails, FX, market rates, settlementReused as-is.
MonetizationSame take-rate model on the transfer.
What's actually differentConfiguration & presentation: many→one contributions, preset/custom amounts, an individual recipient, a branded page.
Verdict: a variation, not a new product — but a strong enough one to package and name as its own use case: "Collect for occasions / group gifting."

That distinction matters. Calling it a new product would mean duplicated flows and roadmap sprawl. Calling it a variation means it ships as configuration on top of an engine that already exists — faster, cheaper, and consistent.

Same engine, new layer

✓ Reused from Collect
  • Hosted payment link, no integration
  • Payer chooses currency at Afriex market rate
  • Payer chooses channel (bank, mobile money, wallet, card)
  • Predictable settlement to the recipient
＋ New in this variation
  • Many payers contributing to one link
  • Preset gift tiers + custom amount
  • Individual (P2P) recipient, not a business
  • Branded, emotional page with a note & messages

The four product deltas

👥
Many → one contributions
The link isn't a single invoice; it's a pot. That introduces a running total, a contributor count, and optional goal semantics — plus a payout of the aggregate to the couple.
🎚️
Preset + custom amounts
Suggested tiers lower the "how much?" friction; a custom field respects generosity and budget. The amount is payer-entered, not fixed by an invoice.
🧑
Individual recipient
The payee is a person, not a KYB'd entity — so limits, verification and compliance posture differ, and payout is to a personal account/wallet.
💌
Branded, human page
Names, photo, a heartfelt note and space for guest messages. The checkout carries emotion, not just a number.

The flow

  1. Open the couple's pageA branded landing with names, date, Nairobi, a note and the running total of gifts received so far.
  2. Choose a giftPreset tiers ($25 to $500) or a custom amount — always shown in the couple's base currency.
  3. Pay in your own currency & channelThe guest picks NGN, KES, GHS, ZAR (etc.) at the Afriex market rate, then a supported channel.
  4. Add a note & sendAn optional message to the couple, then confirm. The total updates and the guest joins the contributor count.
